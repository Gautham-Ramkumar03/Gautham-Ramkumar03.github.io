What was wrong

A LiDAR answers one question well: where is something. On a construction site that is not enough, in two directions.

A grass bank or a speed breaker rises out of the ground, so the robot stops for something it could drive over. Wet concrete and mud are flat, so the robot treats them as road.

And there is a quieter problem. Every sensor has a blind area close to the machine. A pallet that was in plain view from a distance can be invisible when it matters.

What the robot can do now

  • Split ground from obstacle on every scan, on slopes and rough ground, not only on a flat floor.
  • Remember. Out of sight does not mean gone. An obstacle that leaves the sensor’s view is kept in the robot’s picture for a while instead of vanishing with the last scan.
  • Catch low things. Pallets and curbs, which are easy to mistake for uneven ground.
  • Fit through tight places. Where space is short, the robot checks its real outline, not a generous box.
  • Help during manual driving too. The same obstacle check can stop the machine when a person is driving it remotely.

Adding meaning

Geometry cannot tell mud from road. In the IROS 2026 workshop paper we keep LiDAR as the safety signal and let a camera correct it only where the camera is confident and the LiDAR is known to be weak. It runs in real time on an embedded GPU. We released the dataset with it, recorded on active construction sites. A second paper, on learning what is drivable without hand labels, is under review at ICRA 2027.

Next

Planned, not done. I wrote the design for the next perception layer: one camera network that answers several questions at once, such as what a surface is, who is around, and how hard the ground is to drive. As a first check I measured a segmentation model of similar size running in real time on the computer already in the robot. The data pipeline is built; the model itself is not trained yet.
