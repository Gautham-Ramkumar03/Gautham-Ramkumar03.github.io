Mapping

Build a map of a working site that everything else can stand on

14x denser maps
0.7 to 0.04 localization fitness score, lower is better
15 m to 0.4 m return-to-start height error, 2 km drive

What was wrong

The first mapper was odometry with nothing behind it. Whatever error it collected on a drive stayed in the map. Every taught route needed its own map, and a mapping session could not run much longer than half an hour.

Everything else in the stack matches against the map. A map that is slightly wrong makes a robot that is slightly lost, everywhere, forever.

What the robot can do now

  • Map a site in pieces. Each drive is a session. Sessions are joined into one map, so a site can grow one area at a time and nobody has to drive it all in one go.
  • Close its own loops, carefully. When the robot comes back to a place it has seen, the map is corrected. A wrong correction bends the whole map, so a correction has to pass independent checks before it is accepted.
  • Leave people out of it. Moving people and vehicles are filtered out, so they are much less likely to end up as walls.
  • Map large sites on a small computer. Working memory is bounded instead of growing with the map.
  • Tell the operator. Mapping is started, watched and stopped from the operator’s screen, and a poor session is refused with a reason instead of quietly making a bad map.

How

The mapper is LiDAR-inertial odometry in front of a factor-graph back end (GTSAM, iSAM2) with loop closure. Joining sessions uses feature-based global registration and scan matching. It supports two families of LiDAR from one codebase.

How I know

  • Maps came out about 14 times denser than the old mapper’s.
  • The fitness score localization reports against the map (a scan-matching score, lower is better, not a position error) fell from 0.7-1.2 to 0.006-0.04.
  • The half-hour limit is gone.
  • On one 2 km drive the old result had climbed about 15 m in height by the time the robot was back at its start. After the repair, reprocessing the same drive closes to 0.4 m.

That last one is not finished. A stretch the robot drives only once can still carry a height error of several metres, and that is what the next step is for.

Next

Planned, not done: satellite positioning inside the mapper itself, for long featureless stretches, and a map that updates as the site changes.
