NoMaD Visual Navigation

Monocular Vision-Only Localization and Navigation with NoMaD

Deep LearningFoundation ModelsROS2

Problem

Our robots normally localize and map using LiDAR, wheel odometry, and RTK GPS. The question we wanted to answer: can a monocular camera and the NoMaD model replace that stack for reliable route-following, or not? In other words, is learned vision-only navigation a viable substitute for classical localization and mapping on our platform?

Approach

The work builds on:

  • NoMaD: Goal Masked Diffusion Policies for Navigation and Exploration, Sridhar et al. (project page)
  • visualnav-transformer, the reference implementation (github.com/robodhruv/visualnav-transformer)

Porting NoMaD to Our Robot

NoMaD navigates by matching current camera observations against a topological map (a sequence of images from a prior traversal) and uses a diffusion policy to predict the next waypoint toward the closest topomap node. We extended the reference implementation to run on our in-house mobile robot: bridging camera and odometry topics into the NoMaD stack, wiring a PD controller on top of the predicted waypoints, and packaging the whole flow behind scripts for environment setup, data collection, training, and inference.

End-to-End Research Pipeline

One manual demonstration drive produces a rosbag, which is processed into a topological map and training data. The trained model is then evaluated on the same route to study how well vision-only diffusion-policy navigation generalizes outside the original NoMaD benchmarks.

Findings

On our robot, NoMaD was able to follow learned routes using only a monocular camera and learned odometry, with no LiDAR, GPS, or metric map. That is the feasibility answer we were looking for. The experiments also surfaced practical limits of a vision-only stack (sensitivity to lighting and viewpoint drift, tuning effort for the diffusion sampler, and failure modes under novel obstacles), which is useful signal for deciding when a classical localization and mapping stack is still the right call.
