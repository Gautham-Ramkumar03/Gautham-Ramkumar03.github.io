A robot nobody is watching

Flo Mobility’s robots carry material across construction sites and factory floors. They go indoors and outdoors, through dust, slopes, parked trucks and people who are busy with their own work. Most of the time nobody is watching them.

That last part decides everything. A demo robot can fail and be reset. A robot that works alone has to notice its own mistakes and recover from them, because nobody is there to reset it. Flo Cortex is the software that does that, and one codebase runs every robot Flo ships: a new machine is a new configuration, not a new stack.

My part in it

When I joined as an intern in November 2024, the robots navigated on RTK GPS alone. Under the guidance of Manoj Karnekar, I built the stack’s first 3D LiDAR mapping and localization, so the robots could keep working where satellites could not reach. Since going full-time in May 2025 I have taken it further: mapping and localization rebuilt on factor graphs, GPS and LiDAR fused into one estimate, and a perception layer from LiDAR and cameras. Today I write most of the mapping, localization and control in the stack, and I lead its development: what gets built, in what order, and what is ready to go on a robot.

The stack has five layers. Each one has its own page, in the order a robot meets them on a site. The work shown on them is my own. Every page says what the robot could not do, what it can do now, and how I know.

Next

Planned, not done. The storyboard in the gallery shows the whole picture: a robot that is shown a site once, understands it, takes jobs in plain words and keeps its own map current.

  • Vision in the estimator. Visual-inertial odometry as one more source for localization, for the places where LiDAR has little to hold on to.
  • Control that learns. Reinforcement learning alongside the model-based controller, for the manoeuvres skilled operators do better than a cost function.
  • Maps that stay alive. A map that updates as the site changes, instead of one that is rebuilt.
  • Perception that names things. Camera models that tell the robot what an obstacle is, not only where.
